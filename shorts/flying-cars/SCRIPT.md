# "The Day Everyone Got a Flying Car" — script v1
Format: ~60 s vertical short, ~3.7 words/s, second-person mode.

## Facts (check before voice)
- ~1.19 million road deaths a year worldwide (WHO Global Status Report on Road Safety 2023).
- Private pilot licence needs at least 40 flight hours (FAA 14 CFR 61.109).
- US has over 200 million licensed drivers (FHWA).
- ~14,000 FAA air traffic controllers guide ~45,000 flights a day in the US.
- Aircraft hit 17,000+ birds a year in the US (FAA Wildlife Strike Database).
- A helicopter in hover burns many times the fuel of a car going the same time (hover = fighting gravity, no coasting).

## Voice-over
If everyone got a flying car tomorrow, the sky would be a traffic jam by lunch.
It's Monday morning. You press a button, your driveway drops away, and so does everyone else's.
Congratulations, you're now sharing the air with two hundred million licensed drivers.
To fly a plane, you need at least forty hours of training. To drive, you needed a short test and one parallel park you barely survived.
Now the guy who never uses his turn signal has three whole dimensions to not use it in.
Road rage gets altitude. Honking gets worse, because now it comes from above.
The birds are furious. Planes already hit over seventeen thousand of them a year, and now they're dodging your cousin Kevin.
Luckily, nobody has to parallel park anymore.
Unluckily, now you have to parallel park in three dimensions.
Wait, so why doesn't this already work with planes?
You see, planes aren't safe because pilots are great. They're safe because about fourteen thousand controllers give every flight its own lane and altitude.
Think of the mall food court at noon. Now give every stranger a jetpack.
And when a car breaks down, it rolls to the shoulder. A flying car just becomes a very expensive lawn dart.
So the fix is invisible roads in the sky, speed limits, traffic lights and fines.
We'd spend billions to invent a flying car, then use it to rebuild the road.
Only now the potholes are a lot farther down.
